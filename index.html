Project 2 · Solo

Sleepwalker,
walk in your sleep

Rest Stop · asleep
CSCI 4980 Solo Unity 6 C# 2D physics
Play in browser

A 2D platformer where you can only stand on what you can't see. Hold Shift to fall asleep: the screen fades to black and blue dream blocks become solid, but you can no longer see them. Memorize the level with your eyes open, then cross it blind.

Yellow waking blocks only exist while you are awake, and a ring shows how far you can sleepwalk before you wake up, so every room asks where to close your eyes and when to open them.

What I built

  • The core mechanic: sleep state, dream and waking blocks, the darkness fade, and the memory ring
  • Player movement with coyote time and a slower sleepwalking speed
  • Seven rooms that each use the mechanic differently, plus title and win screens
  • A playtest script and survey, run with classmates in class
  • An updated prototype built from that feedback

Seven rooms, one mechanic

Each room introduces one new way to use sleep, with at most two lines of hint text.

  1. Doze. Close your eyes and the dream bridge holds you.
  2. Stairs. Jump in your sleep from memory.
  3. Rest Stop. The crossing is longer than the ring. Wake up halfway.
  4. Walls. Sleepwalk straight through yellow walls.
  5. Blink. Fall asleep and wake up in mid-air on every jump.
  6. Trapdoor. Fall through a dream floor awake, then sleep to be caught.
  7. Home. Everything at once.

From playtest to update

  • "I kept slipping off the steps." Swapped the rounded collider for a flat one with soft corners, and widened the steps.
  • "If you sleep and jump at the same time, you can't jump." Added coyote time, as the tester suggested.
  • "Still confused regarding the ring." The ring now turns red near its edge and flashes when it wakes you, and the bridges are too long to jump over.
  • "Missing the blocks while I was asleep." Sleepwalking is slower, so blind jumps are easier to aim.

The Legend of Zelda,
Level 1 rebuilt

Boss · Aquamentus
CSCI 4980 Unity 6 C#
Play in browser Source on GitHub

A recreation of the first dungeon from the original 1986 Legend of Zelda: grid-based movement, room-by-room camera transitions, locked doors and keys, every enemy in the Eagle dungeon, and the Aquamentus boss fight.

On top of the recreation, we designed an original seven-room level built around a new parry-and-dash mechanic.

What I built

  • Health, damage, hit-stun and invincibility frames shared by Link and every enemy
  • Room transitions, locked doors and keys, and shutter doors that seal until a room is cleared
  • Stalfos, Keese, Gel, Goriya, Wallmaster, and the Aquamentus boss
  • Authentic enemy and item placement and full dungeon progression
  • The seven-room custom level, armored enemies, and the golden boss

Custom level: Parry & Dash

Parry an attack at the last moment to take no damage. Three parries power up the sword. Dash to slip straight through enemies and hazards. Armored enemies shrug off normal hits, so the player has to choose between standing their ground and getting out of the way.

  1. Parry training. The exit only opens after your first power-up.
  2. Dash training. Armored guards block the only corridor.
  3. Projectiles. Parry Goriya boomerangs to charge up.
  4. Blade gauntlet. Wait for the blades, or dash through them.
  5. Arena. Everything at once for the boss key.
  6. Golden Aquamentus. Only a powered-up sword breaks its armor.
  7. Victory. Claim the Triforce.
